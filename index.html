Our vision

A new team, built inside a hospital.

Everyday Omics is being established at Tel Aviv Sourasky Medical Center. We sit between the sequencer and the bedside: we build models that read genomes, and the evidence layer that makes what they say usable in care.

Most of what genomics can already do never reaches the people it was meant to help. Predictions arrive uncalibrated, evidence arrives without a source, and what lands in the chart is a report nobody can act on. Closing that distance — between a measurement and a decision someone is willing to defend — is the work.

Everything we build is meant to run in a working hospital, on real patients, in the time a clinician actually has.

Research

Three questions we keep running into.

  1. “What does this variant actually do?”

    Genomic foundation models, beyond missense

    Clinically, most variants are not missense — and most predictors are. We build and benchmark ensembles of genomic foundation models across regulatory, splicing and non-coding variation, and we treat calibration and honest benchmarking as part of the result rather than an appendix to it.

  2. “What should anyone do about it?”

    Evidence that survives contact with the clinic

    A score is not a decision. We build systems that assemble structured evidence with its provenance intact — ACMG criteria, pharmacogenomic guidance, polygenic risk — and render it as something a clinician can defend in a room and a patient can actually follow.

  3. “Can we know it earlier, without a needle?”

    Genomes read from cell-free DNA

    Fetal and tumour genomes leave traces in circulating blood. Building on Bayesian and deep-learning approaches to noninvasive prenatal diagnosis of monogenic disorders, we develop methods that recover genotypes accurately enough to act on — without an invasive procedure.
